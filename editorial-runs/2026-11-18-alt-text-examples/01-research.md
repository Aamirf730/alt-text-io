# Research handoff

Research checked 2026-11-18. This file is for the planner, writer and implementer.

## Writing approach

- Explicit brief: original, practical plain English with concrete visual examples. Use approximately 1,800 to 2,500 words of article prose plus examples. Skip semicolons and en and em dashes in all visible copy. Use familiar search wording naturally, with one jump link per section. Give each card a visual, a short context, Weak and Better variants and a one-sentence reason.
- Publication sample: the opening of [the how-to guide](../../frontend-next/src/app/how-to-write-alt-text/page.tsx) addresses the reader directly and uses short declarative sentences. Its Paris café example changes the point of focus when it changes the article context. Its WordPress steps name the particular editor and field the reader uses. Match those traits. Keep that page the reference for general rules, with one concise illustrative example and a contextual link to this library where appropriate. Keep its existing section ID to preserve deep links.
- Secondary examples of house voice: [the checker](../../frontend-next/src/app/alt-text-checker/page.tsx) uses familiar labels and gives a specific next action for a finding. [The WooCommerce page](../../frontend-next/src/app/woocommerce-product-image-alt-text/page.tsx) distinguishes what a product photo shows from what the seller must confirm in their catalog.
- Tone and formality: conversational and precise. Skip invented personal authority and promotional flourishes. A good explanation names exactly what the missing text would cost a reader.
- Technical depth: assume someone can edit a page or media entry, but define an HTML attribute, an accessible name and the distinction between an image in HTML and one applied by CSS as they come up. Explain what is announced with a short example. Keep deeper markup in real, copyable snippets. Give Web and WordPress readers a shared starting point without repeating the procedural how-to.
- Point of view: Alt Text Pro publishes the guide. Speak directly to a website editor, writer, designer or shop owner as “you.” Use the third person for imagined scenarios and use Alt Text Pro by name for the product CTA. No assignment of identity, emotion or professional role to image subjects without evidence. The scenarios may be fictional if marked illustrative.

## Asset and example inventory

I inspected reduced copies of the three local demo photographs. Each full-size original is 2816 by 1536. The site's own Git history, commit `7d5763f`, treats these as homepage demo assets. The two suggested photo scenarios must read as hypothetical illustration, never as reporting from a verified event or place.

1. Photos: use `/business-team.jpg` twice. It shows eight people around a long, natural-finish conference table, with two people standing by a whiteboard in a glass office. On a hypothetical workplace news page, focus on what people are doing. On a product page for the table, focus on the long top and room to seat people with laptops. Do not give material or dimensions without a catalog fact. For a place photo use `/mountain-landscape.jpg`, with snow-striped peaks and evergreens reflected in a calm lake. Do not name the mountain or lake. Reserve the Paris café scene for the current how-to to avoid overlap.
2. Logos: draw a compact fictional mark and name, such as Alder & Finch, with ample whitespace. Reuse the asset for an unlinked brand mark, a home link and a partner strip. The strip should visually identify at least one partner and its alt treatment should match a strip with or without equivalent visible names as actually implemented. A minor naming collision can be avoided with coined names and no existing brand identity or design.
3. Icons: use a visible-label-and-icon presentation, an icon-only named control or link, and a decorative flourish. Lucide shapes work. A local SVG used through `<img>` can show copyable HTML, while an inline Lucide SVG can be hidden and named at the parent control as applicable.
4. Buttons and links: draw or reuse a cart or continuation graphic. Show the actual nesting of the working code in a snippet. A preview link can point to a real on-page destination or be clearly presented as a specimen; avoid dead links and focusable buttons with no behavior. Give an image submit example as a second snippet or card if practical.
5. Chart: draw an SVG with three bars for a single illustrative measure. For example, April 48, May 62 and June 76 repair bookings. Label the graphic and table fictional and keep axis labels, values and visual proportions consistent. Publish the values in a real table.
6. Product: draw a blue ceramic mug with a broad curved handle in side and top-down views. Clearly label the visual as an illustration of a fictional product. Treat type, color and ceramic construction as known from the sample catalog, and write each Better sentence for what that view adds.
7. Screenshot: draw a screenshot-style crop with one readable state such as “Image settings” with “Alternative text” open. Avoid implying it captures a specific WordPress version if it is a mockup. Put the underlying instruction in regular text.
8. Images of text: draw a simple sample quote card or social graphic. All intended wording, dates or attribution that appears only in the card should be transcribed correctly. Avoid fictitious quotation attribution.
9. Decoration and backgrounds: use a local ornamental asset with native `alt=""` and a separate CSS example with a visible text equivalent for meaningful information. Keep the image-and-attribute snippet attached to the decorative HTML example.
10. SEO: use `/fresh-vegetables.jpg` or reuse a distinct photo without repeating its description. The fresh vegetables image shows produce stacked in market stalls with carrots and tomatoes prominent. A natural description can name these only as needed. Keep weak stuffing visible as text rather than real alt markup.

The asset names and topics here are recommendations. Coordinate any changes to visible words, chart data, or actual Better alt across the draft and page. Every meaningful asset shown as the subject needs the final Better value in its rendered markup. Include explicit intrinsic dimensions on each `<img>` or Next Image. A CSS background has no `img` element to label. Declare two or more intentional empty alt cases with a visible explanation, and test all live markup for accessible control names.

## Editorial and release implications

- The visual comparison library is **Strong** as a distinct editorial format. The current guide has its own small examples section, so the new article should move beyond rules into reusable cards and leave the useful how-to explanation in place. Every card should still make sense with its image hidden.
- The keyword research supplied by the brief is planning input. The ten sections give reader tasks that can be answered directly. The predicted traffic or ability to win a snippet is **Promising but Unproven**. Keyword Planner competition refers to advertising, and the reported Bing snapshot does not establish organic difficulty or current placement. Leave these values and comparisons out of the article. A cheap early search test is to establish a Search Console baseline after indexing and review queries, impressions and clicks for the new route before expanding the series.
- The checker makes its named/empty/missing distinctions from server HTML. Its fetching helper blocks localhost by design. For local verification, exercise the existing classifier on a fetched local page and, if end-to-end verification is needed, use an isolated harness that serves only this local fixture through the normal checker UI. No security rule needs changing. [Fetcher](../../netlify-functions/utils/safe-fetch.js)
- The local baseline has no route-level Open Graph image files. The implementer should recheck Git before taking the specified fallback of a 1200 by 630 route image.
- Use a descriptive anchor from the new article to the existing writing guide and a final short Sources list linking WAI Images Tutorial and Decision Tree, WCAG 2.2 SC 1.1.1, the HTML alt requirements and Google Search Central. Keep metadata, links and example copy synchronized. Record the publication date based on genuine publication as part of release, and keep the feature branch local until publication is authorized.
